# Noto tight-road-budget DDA sensitivity

## Purpose

This diagnostic tests whether the repaired support-preserving DDA becomes policy-active when the road-retrofit budget is scarce. All exposure, capacity, cost, failure, travel-time, ambiguity-radius, and density-cap inputs are held fixed. Only the road budget `B_Y` changes.

The primary capped-TV specification uses `kappa=2`, the five-level road grid `{0, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1}`, and `rho in {0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25}`.

## Resolution-robustness update

The budget-frontier policy switches reported below occur on the five-level grid. A follow-up grid with 0.05 increments on the two critical links evaluates 313 feasible policies at every radius while retaining the full five-link state and recourse model. At the midpoint of the strongest coarse-grid interval, the refined grid selects `[0, 1, 0.2, 0, 0]` for every radius and produces `Delta_rho=0` throughout.

Accordingly, the coarse-grid switch is a discretization-sensitive diagnostic, not evidence that DDA changes the empirical Noto road-retrofit policy. The coarse results remain useful for showing why policy claims must be checked against decision resolution.

## Exact checkpoint reuse

`B_Y` is a separate road-feasibility constraint. For a fixed road vector `y`, changing `B_Y` does not alter the fixed-`y` optimization over building renovation and capacity, the nominal distribution, the worst-case distribution, or the objective. A tighter budget only removes road vectors from the feasible set.

The analysis therefore filters the completed 5,976 exact fixed-`y` evaluations. Every policy retained by the tighter budget was evaluated previously, and archive completeness is checked before any table is written. Two policies from the strongest mechanism-active case were also independently re-solved; both objectives reproduced the archive exactly.

## Requested 0.4 and 0.5 multipliers

The original road budget is `B_Y=2.296738`. The suggested reductions produce the following discretized optima.

| Budget multiplier | New `B_Y` | Feasible grid policies | Best `y`, all `rho` values | Policy change | `Delta_0.25` |
|---:|---:|---:|:---|:---:|---:|
| 0.40 | 0.918695 | 57 | `[0, 1, 0, 0, 0]` | No | 0 |
| 0.50 | 1.148369 | 113 | `[0, 1, 0.25, 0, 0]` | No | 0 |

Thus, the two proposed cutoffs do not activate DDA. At 0.40, the budget is just sufficient to fully harden the second link, which dominates every diversified grid policy at every ambiguity radius. At 0.50, the planner can fully harden that link and allocate 0.25 to the third link, and this policy also remains optimal for every radius.

## Exhaustive budget frontier

To avoid selecting a favorable budget informally, the analysis evaluates every distinct road-budget threshold represented by the 996 archived feasible grid policies. These thresholds collapse into 23 policy phases. Six phases are DDA-active.

The strongest phase is:

`0.469420 <= B_Y multiplier < 0.479479`.

At its lower endpoint, `B_Y=1.078135`:

| `rho` | Best discretized `y` | Best objective | Objective using nominal policy | `Delta_rho` |
|---:|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.00 | `[0, 0.5, 0.75, 0, 0]` | 5280.690 | 5280.690 | 0.000 |
| 0.05 | `[0, 1, 0, 0, 0]` | 5317.397 | 5339.412 | 22.016 |
| 0.10 | `[0, 1, 0, 0, 0]` | 5346.068 | 5389.883 | 43.815 |
| 0.15 | `[0, 1, 0, 0, 0]` | 5374.557 | 5440.038 | 65.481 |
| 0.20 | `[0, 1, 0, 0, 0]` | 5402.348 | 5490.171 | 87.824 |
| 0.25 | `[0, 1, 0, 0, 0]` | 5427.630 | 5533.503 | 105.873 |

At `rho=0.25`, using the ambiguity-dependent policy rather than retaining the nominal policy reduces the robust objective by 105.873, or 1.951% of the optimized robust objective. This is a meaningful policy-and-value effect on the discretized grid.

## Mechanism diagnostic

Under the coarse-grid nominal policy `[0, 0.5, 0.75, 0, 0]`, the nominal probability that both critical links fail is 0.031605. At every positive radius in the strongest phase, the capped adversary raises that joint-failure probability to 0.063211, exactly twice its nominal value. State-level diagnostics show that the largest probability additions are assigned more broadly to high-loss states containing Link 2; the joint Link 2-Link 3 failure is only one component of this shift.

The ambiguity-dependent policy fully hardens the second link. The nominal and worst-case probabilities of both critical links failing then become zero. No probability is assigned outside nominal support, so the policy switch is generated by the intended support-preserving mechanism rather than by resurrection of an impossible failure state.

However, the refined nominal optimum already fully hardens Link 2 and assigns 0.2 to Link 3. The coarse policy comparison therefore does not survive the finer decision grid.

## Interpretation for the paper

The baseline empirical road result remains retrofit-policy stability. The 0.40 and 0.50 budget experiments and the refined active-budget grid are also stable in `y`. None should be presented as evidence that DDA changes the empirical Noto retrofit decision. A later moment-aware analysis identifies a small capacity reallocation in `w`; see `docs/noto_moment_ambiguity_results.md`.

The exhaustive five-level budget frontier may be reported as a resolution diagnostic: coarse discretization creates narrow policy-active intervals, but those intervals disappear in the refined critical-link test. This strengthens the methodological conclusion that optimizer changes must be accompanied by grid-resolution or continuous verification.

The strongest coarse phase was identified ex post by a complete threshold scan. It is not a newly calibrated empirical baseline, and its 1.951% switching value should not be used as the paper's main DDA mechanism claim.
