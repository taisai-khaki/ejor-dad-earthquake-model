## Data Gathering

Because the Nepal/KLL earthquake survey data were not accessible, the empirical study was shifted to the 2023 Türkiye earthquake. The selected Turkey stack is suitable because it is public, spatially explicit, and combines observed/post-event damage with population, roads, and facility layers needed by the model.

### Sources

- Zenodo record 18437501, *Building damage datasets for the 2023 Türkiye earthquake*: used for building damage labels, building footprints, and hazard/influencing-factor rasters such as PGV, fault distance, epicenter distance, DEM, and lithology.
- HDX/HOTOSM Turkey destroyed buildings: used as a GIS-ready post-event destroyed-building layer and external damage reference.
- HDX/HOTOSM Turkey health facilities: used to locate candidate emergency/aid centers.
- OpenStreetMap roads, accessed through Overpass/Geofabrik-compatible schema: used to construct the road network, route times, and road-link candidates.
- WorldPop Turkey 2020 constrained UN-adjusted population raster: aggregated to model zones to estimate exposed population.
- Official/unit-cost sources are used only as broad calibration anchors; the reported experiments primarily use scenario budget fractions.

### Preparation

- Downloaded only the necessary Zenodo components instead of requiring the full archive.
- Loaded `This_study` damage shapefiles for the selected city-level study areas.
- Built regular grid zones because uniform grids are reproducible and do not require restricted administrative survey boundaries.
- Assigned each building/damage feature to a grid zone.
- Computed zone-level population by aggregating the WorldPop raster to the same grid.
- Selected health facilities near the damage zones and mapped facility type to capacity proxies.
- Pulled OSM roads around the affected area and computed shortest-path route structure between centers and zones.
- Selected candidate road links from frequently used center-zone routes.
- Enriched each candidate link with road class, bridge indicator, destroyed-building proximity, and sampled PGV/fault/epicenter raster values.

## Variable Construction

### Directly observed or derived

- `P_rl`: zone population, aggregated from WorldPop raster cells.
- `q_rl`: severe/collapse fraction, computed from Zenodo building damage labels.
- `D(0)`: baseline at-risk population, computed as `sum_rl P_rl q_rl`.
- `K`: emergency centers, selected from HOTOSM health facilities.
- `L`: road network, derived from OSM roads.
- `tau_krl^s`: route travel-time proxy from OSM shortest-path travel times and state-specific road disruption penalties.

### Scenario-calibrated

- `Phi_ij`: link failure probability. It is not directly observed; it is constructed from road class, bridge/critical-road indicator, destroyed-building corridor exposure, and hazard rasters.
- `C_rl`: zone renovation cost proxy, based on building counts/exposure.
- `C_ij`: road retrofit cost proxy, based on road length, road class, and hazard/terrain multipliers.
- `lambda_k`: capacity expansion unit cost proxy, based on facility type.
- `w_k^0`: existing capacity proxy, based on facility type.
- `B_Z`, `B_Y`, `B_X`: budget levels, expressed as scenario fractions and tested through sensitivity analysis.
- `rho`: ambiguity radius, tested from 0.00 to 0.25.

## Model and Algorithm Adjustments

State these adjustments clearly in the model/computational section:

- The original model is implemented as a computational DR-DAD framework with zone renovation `z`, road retrofit `y`, and capacity expansion `w`.
- Regular grid zones replace administrative Nepal survey zones because the Turkey case relies on open geospatial layers rather than a household survey geography.
- The road-failure uncertainty set is compacted to selected candidate links and states with up to two simultaneous candidate-link failures plus a tail state.
- For a fixed road-retrofit vector `y`, the recourse/renovation/capacity evaluation is solved exactly over the constructed state space.
- The current all-sector solution is a feasible incumbent for the road-retrofit problem, not a certified global optimum.
- The continuous SBB certificate is not used for the Turkey paper results; the support-preserving SBB repair is evaluated in the separate Noto analysis. Present the Turkey results as scenario-calibrated empirical optimization, not theorem-level global optimality.
- Travel-time disruptions are route-based approximations: failure states penalize routes containing disrupted candidate links rather than simulating a full dynamic road-network rerouting model.

## Result-Section Structure

Suggested subsection order:

1. Empirical setting and data stack: explain why Turkey replaces Nepal and list the open data sources.
2. Instance construction: describe zones, population, damage fractions, facilities, roads, and candidate link selection.
3. Calibration: explain observed vs constructed vs scenario-calibrated variables.
4. Main policy results: present Table 3 and Figure 1.
5. Sector contribution: present Table 4; emphasize building dominance and road marginal gain.
6. Robustness: present ambiguity, budget-design, and candidate-link sensitivity.
7. Limitations and interpretation: state that the data calibrate exposure/damage and support a scenario study, not complete validation of every model variable.

## Claims to Use

- The empirical layer validates/calibrates the exposure and building-damage component of the model.
- The road-disruption layer is a defensible scenario generator, not observed link-failure truth.
- Public Turkey data provide a reproducible validation setting when the original Nepal data are inaccessible.
- The results favor the paper's mechanism: integrated mitigation improves worst-case expected outcomes, and road retrofits matter more when the disrupted-road candidate set is enriched with hazard exposure.

## Claims to Avoid

- Do not say the survey/data validate the whole model.
- Do not say `Phi_ij` is observed.
- Do not say emergency capacity and mitigation costs are observed.
- Do not claim global optimality for the all-sector continuous road-retrofit solution.
- Do not imply the Turkey case reproduces the Nepal/KLL survey design.
